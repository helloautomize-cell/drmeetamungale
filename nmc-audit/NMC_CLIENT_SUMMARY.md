# What the new NMC rules mean for Mungale Eye Hospital

**For: Dr. Meeta Mungale & Dr. Sachin Mungale — prepared 26 October 2026**

The National Medical Commission has published new guidelines on how doctors and hospitals may present themselves publicly — website, Google profile, social media, everything. Most of it is a list of things that are no longer allowed, regardless of good intentions or patient consent. We audited the full website against these rules. The site is in good shape on the basics — both doctors' Gujarat Medical Council numbers are displayed, every treatment page carries honest risk information, the appointment form has proper consent checkboxes, and there are no hidden trackers or pop-ups. But we found several things that must change.

## The most urgent fix — patient photographs

The Gallery and About pages currently show real patients. Several photos clearly show patients' faces during examinations — and two photos show a computer screen with a patient's **name, record number and date of birth** visible. Under the new rules, patient photos may only be published when they are fully anonymous (no face, no identifying details) **and** you hold written consent. Patient record screens must never be photographed. We recommend removing every photo that contains a patient or a clinical screen immediately. If you want any kept, they need a signed consent letter per patient and must show no face.

## What else we found — and what we will change

**Patient reviews ("Patient stories").** The homepage section showing Google reviews, the five-star ratings, and the embedded Google quote are not permitted — patient testimonials are banned for promotion even when genuine and even with the patient's consent. We will remove the section and keep a simple "Find us on Google" link.

**Numbers about surgeries.** The "500+ corneal transplants" figure and the "90% success rate" line on the glaucoma page are both prohibited — statistics like these count as promotional claims, verified or not. We will replace them with your real credentials: LVPEI fellowships, publications, teaching roles. These carry the same trust value.

**Claims like "state-of-the-art", "gold standard", "first installation in Vadodara", "one of the largest theatres in the city", "cutting-edge".** Comparisons and superlatives are banned unless independently verifiable. We will rewrite them as plain, factual descriptions — which read better anyway and still say everything true about the hospital.

**Old promotional lines inside blog articles.** A few older posts contain lines like "your premier choice" and "renowned throughout Vadodara". Articles stay protected as patient education only while they remain genuinely educational, so we will clean those sentences out.

**"Built & Marketed by Automize Media Labs".** The new rules say a doctor may not publicly engage a third party to market medical services, so this credit needs to come off the footer (a neutral "Website: …" line is acceptable if the contract requires one).

**AI-made images.** Some artwork and blog covers on the site are AI-generated. That is allowed, but it must be disclosed in the editorial policy and labelled. We will add a short AI-disclosure paragraph and swap the one cover that shows a before/after-style eye image, since that could be read as a promised result.

**Doctor photos.** One portrait per doctor on the profile and directory pages is fine. We'll remove the small doctor photos from the homepage hero and from the social-share image, where they read as advertising rather than identification.

**Small housekeeping items.** The five before/after images and a few patient photos exist on the server even though they aren't shown on pages — we'll move them out of the public folder until consents exist. We'll also add both GMC numbers to the footer and tweak the booking form so patients are asked not to type medical details into it.

## What we need from you

1. **Consent records** — do you hold written consent from any patient whose photo is on the site? If yes for specific photos, tell us which; if no, they all come down.
2. **The "first installation" claim** — only if Remidio has put it in writing; otherwise it goes. (We recommend removing it regardless.)
3. **Which images are AI-made** — so the disclosure wording is accurate.
4. **Your agency agreement** — whether a neutral "Website:" credit is required contractually.
5. **Your social accounts** — please check Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Practo and your Google profile against the same rules: no testimonials, review requests, before/afters, offers, or superlatives; your GMC number on promotional posts. We can help review these if you'd like.

## The overall picture

None of this changes what the hospital does — it changes how the website talks about it. The compliant version is actually closer to how you practise: factual, calm, and credential-led. The only item that carries real regulatory risk today is the patient photography; everything else is a focused copy and component clean-up. Once the images and the claims above are corrected, the site will sit comfortably inside the new guidelines.
